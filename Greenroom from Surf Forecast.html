Hossegor, France

Summer in France means days stretching from 6am dawn patrols to sunset sessions at 10pm. The waves tend to run fun-sized, with offshores in the morning and sea breezes in the afternoon. Hossegor's many banks just about handle the locals and the tourists, and what you lose in size, you get back in beach culture, beach bars, and beach baguettes.

Mentawais, Indonesia

The Mentawais, off the coast of Sumatra, are the standard other boat trips get measured against. You can score epic surf elsewhere, but no other location matches this chain for both quality and quantity. Waves are never guaranteed on any surf trip, but the odds are stacked more in your favour here than almost anywhere else.

Jeffreys Bay, South Africa

This is a reverse summer holiday, into a South African winter. But with one of the world's great point breaks on your doorstep, other good waves nearby, the Addo Elephant Park and Shamwari Game Reserve a short drive away, and dolphin and whale watching on offer, it's a cold-water summer worth having. You won't come back with a tan, but you might come back with stories.

Costa Rica (all of it)

"Costa Rica offers a completely different vibe," says Portuguese big wave surfer Nic Von Rupp. "It is maybe not as high in terms of quality of waves as in Indonesia, but the vibe is incredible."

Every region in Costa Rica has a standout wave, but it's the variety that makes it one of the great all-rounder summer destinations: punchy, hollow waves over volcanic black sand, easy peelers over pale beaches, and craggy reefs for the slab hunters and twin-fin cruisers alike. Nosara is our pick, but you're unlikely to go wrong anywhere in CR.

Tavarua, Fiji

Tavarua is still one of the few resorts with two genuinely world-class waves on the doorstep. The South Pacific winter is prime season for both Cloudbreak and Restaurants, with swell arriving from the Southern Indian Ocean, the Southern Ocean and the Tasman Sea, delivering consistent surf from June to October. Add the snorkelling, the fishing, the dining, and the famously friendly Fijian welcome, and there's not much left to complain about.

Salina Cruz, Mexico

April to October is southern hemisphere swell season, and Mexico gets swept with wave energy from the south, usually paired with workable wind on sand-bottom, mostly right-hand points. The area holds around 17 lesser-known spots alongside busier ones, including the big wave spot Puerto Escondido, but our favourite is Punta Conejo, one of the best waves in the world when the sand lines up.

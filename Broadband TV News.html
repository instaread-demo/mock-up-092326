Ofcom: One in five UK adults use AI for news

September 15, 2026 00.01 Europe/London By Julian Clover

One in five UK adults are using artificial intelligence services to access news, according to Ofcom’s latest study of news consumption.

The News Report 2026 found that 21% of adults had used an AI app or service for news during the previous month, with ChatGPT the most widely used at 13%.

However, audiences remain cautious about AI-generated journalism. More than half of adults (56%) said they would trust an AI-written news story less than one written by a person.

The findings form part of a wider shift towards online intermediaries as gateways to news. Ofcom found 73% of adults accessed news through services including social media, video-sharing platforms, search engines, aggregators and AI, compared with 57% who used publishers’ websites and apps directly.

Online sources were used for news by 82% of adults during the month, ahead of television at 79%. Of an estimated 72 minutes spent consuming news each day, an average 34 minutes was online and 18 minutes on television.

Social media and video-sharing platforms were used for news by 60% of adults. Facebook remains the most commonly encountered platform for news, at 39%, but YouTube has narrowed the gap, reaching 33%.

Traditional broadcasters nevertheless continue to have substantial reach online. The BBC remains the UK’s most-used news provider, reaching 80% of adults across its own services and third-party platforms. Two-thirds of 16 to 24-year-olds encountered BBC News during the month.

Ofcom also found traditional television retained a particularly important role during elections. Following May’s local, mayoral, Scottish Parliament and Senedd elections, television was used for election news by 62% of those aware of the elections, compared with 33% for online sources.

Ian Macrae, Ofcom’s director of market intelligence, said: “In today’s ‘always on’ environment, many people don’t go direct to the news, the news is coming to them through different online intermediaries, increasingly AI-enabled.”

He said public service media would need to continue evolving their distribution strategies to reach audiences as news discovery increasingly moves to third-party platforms.

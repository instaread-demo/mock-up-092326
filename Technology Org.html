China’s CXMT Reaches 11.95 nm Memory Spacing With Quadruple Patterning

Posted Yesterday

CXMT’s Fifth-Generation DRAM

Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by
0:00 / 3:41
Instaread

China’s leading memory maker has pushed key chip features down to about 12 billionths of a metre by running some manufacturing steps several times over. ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) said on Sunday that the resulting fifth-generation DRAM platform has entered mass production. The company says it gives phone and computer makers another supply option beyond Samsung Electronics, SK Hynix and Micron Technology.

Key Takeaways

  • CXMT says its fifth-generation DRAM platform cuts the spacing of key memory-array features to 11.95 nanometres using quadruple patterning, and it is now in mass production.
  • The company claims at least 50% more gross chip dies per wafer than its fourth-generation platform, measured against an 8-gigabit chip baseline.
  • Two new 24-gigabit LPDDR5X chips, each holding 50% more data than CXMT’s previous equivalents, are already in mass production for smartphones and portable devices.

Packing Cells Closer

DRAM, or dynamic random-access memory, is the short-term working memory that phones, computers and other devices use to run apps and programs. The closer its data-storing structures are packed, the more memory fits on each chip and the more chips come out of each silicon wafer.

The Hefei-based company says it reduced the spacing of key features in the data-storage part of the chip to 11.95 nanometres. It got there with “quadruple patterning,” a process that repeats several manufacturing steps to produce finer circuit patterns.

CXMT says the platform makes more powerful memory at lower cost and with lower power use. It developed the process with computer simulations and joint work with Chinese chip-equipment makers on critical production steps.

“Our process capability is now on par with the most advanced mass-produced nodes out there in the industry,” said Luo Xiaodong, CXMT’s vice president and head of its marketing centre, at the 2026 World Manufacturing Convention in Hefei.

Reading the Dies-per-Wafer Claim

CXMT says the new platform can produce at least 50% more gross chip dies per wafer than its fourth-generation platform, using an 8-gigabit chip as the baseline.

That figure needs context. Gross dies measure the potential number of chips on a wafer before defective units are excluded. The number says nothing about how many chips pass final testing, and CXMT did not disclose yields.

Built for Phones

The first products on the platform are two 24-gigabit LPDDR5X chips. LPDDR5X is a power-saving type of DRAM used mainly in smartphones and other portable electronics.

Each chip holds 50% more data than CXMT’s previous equivalent product, and both are already in mass production. They come in two package formats to suit different phone and portable-device designs.

CXMT, China’s leading DRAM producer, listed on Shanghai’s STAR Market this year. It pitches the new chips as an extra source of supply for electronics makers.

That extra supply arrives during a severe memory squeeze. AI data centres are absorbing a growing share of global memory output, and the largest producers have shifted capacity toward high-bandwidth memory for AI accelerators. Bank analysts estimate that DRAM prices will have risen more than 400% between the start of 2024 and the end of 2026. Phone makers paying those prices have every reason to test a new supplier.

The advance also carries political weight. Since 2022, US export controls on advanced chips and chipmaking equipment have restricted China’s access to some of the tools and software needed at the leading edge. Beijing has answered with a drive to reduce its reliance on foreign semiconductor technology. CXMT’s claims still await independent teardown analysis. If they hold, a Chinese DRAM maker is now producing phone memory at a density US controls were meant to keep out of reach.
